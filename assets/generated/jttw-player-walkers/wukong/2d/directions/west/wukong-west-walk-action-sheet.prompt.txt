Use case: transparent-ready 2D storybook game asset for a Journey to the West learning mini-game.
Character: original, child-safe Sun Wukong (孙悟空), a friendly golden-brown monkey hero with a peach muzzle, bright eyes, a small gold circlet, a short red scarf, an ochre-gold cloud-pattern tunic, curled tail, and a compact red-and-gold Ruyi staff.
Direction: side view facing LEFT (west). Keep the same orthographic camera, scale, proportions, costume layers, palette, signature prop and foot baseline as the other directions. The north row must show the back of the same costume; east and west are true side views, not three-quarter views; south clearly shows the face.

Create exactly one sprite sheet on a perfectly flat #00ff00 chroma-key background, no scenery and no text. Exactly 8 equal portrait cells in a 4 columns x 2 rows grid, final sheet 1536x1024, each cell 384x512, with 12px green gutters. Read cells left-to-right on the top row, then left-to-right on the bottom row. One complete character per cell, centered, never clipped or overlapping, all feet on one shared absolute baseline near the bottom, generous transparent-ready margin, identical camera and lighting.

The first four cells are a clear in-place walk cycle with alternating feet and natural arm swing. The last four cells show a playful staff-action cycle: lift the short Ruyi staff, sweep it safely across the body, point it upward, then settle back to ready pose; no impact or violence.
Frames 1-4 are walk poses in this order: contact, left-foot-forward, passing, right-foot-forward. Frames 5-8 are the action poses in this order: anticipation, action-open, action-peak, settle. The action prop must stay the same size and identity. Keep every frame readable at 36x48 pixels, with crisp dark-brown outline and softly textured children’s storybook fills.

Lighting and style: bright welcoming Chinese-fantasy palette, polished 2D game illustration, even light, no cast shadow. No green pixels on the character. no extra characters, no giant staff, no battle damage, no aggressive expression. Also avoid motion blur, watermark, logo, typography, changing camera zoom, oval or stretched body, duplicate limbs, cropped hands or feet, matte lines, checkerboard or fake transparency.
